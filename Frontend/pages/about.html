Zero-Day attacks remain a serious threat as attackers can use vulnerabilities before an official fix is available for them. So to reduce the risk of attack, early detection of threats, constant monitoring, and quick response are very important.

It is impossible to completely prevent unknown vulnerabilities, so organizations must use several levels of protection: regular updates, security testing, access restrictions, monitoring. Also, each organization should have a prepared plan for responding to attacks, this will lower the chances of successful attack and minimize its impact.

In the future, artificial intelligence may play a big role in this area. There are already examples of using AI to find vulnerabilities in software.

As Tim Freestone noted for TechRepublic: On May 11, Google's Threat Intelligence Group reported the first publicly confirmed working zero-day exploit developed with AI assistance: a Python-based 2FA bypass in a popular open-source web administration tool. (Source: TechRepublic)

But AI can be used not only by defenders, but also by attackers to find and use vulnerabilities faster. So, the development of technology will require constant improvement of cybersecurity methods.
